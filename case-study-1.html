Enterprise Design System

Company Nexus Technologies
Role Head of UX Design
Duration 18 months
Team 6 designers, 4 engineers

The Challenge

Nexus Technologies had grown through acquisition, resulting in 12 separate products with inconsistent user experiences. Each product team had its own design patterns, component libraries, and visual language. Users who worked across multiple tools struggled with the cognitive load of relearning interfaces.

The engineering teams were also feeling the pain — duplicate component code across repositories meant bugs were fixed in one place but persisted in others, and new features took longer to build.

My Approach

I structured the initiative into three phases to build momentum while managing organisational change:

  • Audit & Align (months 1–3): Conducted a comprehensive UI audit across all 12 products, cataloguing over 340 unique components. Facilitated workshops with product, engineering, and design leads to define shared principles and priorities.
  • Foundation & Pilot (months 4–9): Established design tokens (colour, typography, spacing, motion), built a core component library of 48 components, and piloted adoption in two products to validate the system.
  • Scale & Govern (months 10–18): Rolled the system out across all products, created contribution guidelines, and established a governance model with rotating product team representation.

Key Decisions

Several critical decisions shaped the project's success:

  • Federated ownership: Rather than centralising the system under one team, I created a federated model where product teams contributed components. This built buy-in and reduced bottlenecks.
  • Accessibility-first: Every component was built to WCAG 2.1 AA from day one. We automated accessibility testing in CI/CD to prevent regressions.
  • Design tokens as source of truth: All visual decisions flowed from tokens, making theme support (including dark mode) a natural capability rather than a retrofit.

Results

60% Reduction in design debt
40% Faster feature delivery
340→48 Components consolidated
92% Team adoption rate

Reflection

The biggest lesson was that a design system is 20% components and 80% culture. The technical artefacts were the easy part — the real challenge was changing how teams thought about shared ownership, contribution, and consistency. Early investment in workshops, documentation, and visible executive sponsorship was essential.
