Jira Issue View Redesign

Company Atlassian
Role Lead Designer
Duration Jul 2020 – Oct 2021
Team Multiple engineering teams & designers (AU & India)

The Challenge

The issue view is the single most used experience in Jira. It's where millions of users spend the majority of their time — reading, updating, commenting on, and triaging work items. Despite being the product's core surface, the issue view had accumulated years of incremental additions without a holistic redesign.

The result was an experience that felt dense and overwhelming, particularly for newer users, while power users struggled with efficiency. Information hierarchy was unclear, and the view didn't adapt well across different use cases — from a developer quickly updating a status to a project manager reviewing a complex epic.

My Role

As Lead Designer for Jira Shared Experiences, I led the design of this programme of work across multiple engineering teams and designers distributed across Australia and India. My responsibilities included:

  • Setting the design vision and strategy for the new issue view
  • Creating UX design principles, personas, information architecture, flows, wireframes, prototypes, and visual designs
  • Leading design across a programme of work consisting of multiple engineering teams
  • Coordinating with designers in Australia and India to maintain design quality and consistency
  • Setting up user validation tests, card-sorting sessions, A/B tests, focus groups, and user interviews
  • Working with Waterfall and Scrum methodologies together with product owners, business stakeholders, technical architects, and developers

Design Approach

The redesign was guided by several key principles:

  • Progressive disclosure: Surface the most relevant information first. Details, activity, and metadata are organised into clear zones that users can expand based on their current task.
  • Clear information hierarchy: The issue title, status, and description take visual priority. Supporting metadata moves to a structured sidebar that's scannable but not overwhelming.
  • Contextual actions: Rather than showing all possible actions at once, actions appear contextually based on what the user is focusing on.
  • Responsive to use case: The layout adapts whether the issue view is opened in full-page mode, as a side panel, or in a modal — the same content, optimised for each context.

Impact

The redesigned issue view is now the standard experience for all Jira users. As the most frequently used surface in one of the world's most widely used project management tools, the impact touches millions of daily active users. The redesign established patterns that continue to influence how Jira's interface evolves.
