PayFlow Dashboard Redesign

Company PayFlow
Role UX Design Lead
Duration 10 months
Team 4 designers, 8 engineers

The Challenge

PayFlow's merchant dashboard was the primary tool for over 50,000 businesses to manage payments, view transaction data, and handle disputes. But the dashboard had grown organically over five years, accumulating features without a cohesive information architecture. Merchants reported that simple tasks — like finding a specific transaction or initiating a refund — took too many clicks and too much cognitive effort.

Customer support tickets related to "can't find" and "how do I" queries made up 38% of all inbound volume.

Research & Discovery

I led a mixed-methods research initiative to understand merchant workflows:

  • Task analysis with 18 merchants across small, medium, and enterprise segments to map the top 10 critical workflows
  • Card sorting with 40 participants to validate a new information architecture
  • Heuristic evaluation against Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics, revealing major issues in visibility of system status and error prevention
  • Competitive analysis of 6 competing payment platforms to identify opportunities for differentiation

Design Solutions

The redesign focused on three pillars:

  • Contextual navigation: Replaced the monolithic sidebar with a role-based navigation that surfaced relevant tools based on merchant type and recent activity
  • Universal search: Introduced a command-palette-style search that allowed merchants to find transactions, customers, and actions from anywhere in the dashboard
  • Actionable overview: Redesigned the home view as a personalised dashboard showing key metrics, pending actions, and anomaly alerts — not just raw data

Results

45% Faster task completion
-52% "How do I" support tickets
+28 NPS point increase
89% Merchant satisfaction

Reflection

This project taught me the power of progressive disclosure in complex tools. Merchants didn't need fewer features — they needed the right features at the right time. The universal search became the most-loved feature, with 68% of active users adopting it within the first month. It proved that investing in navigational infrastructure can be more impactful than adding new capabilities.
